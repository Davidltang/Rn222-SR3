XENON1TWiki - https://xe1t-wiki.lngs.infn.it/S1-only radon event selection
Dominick Cichon  (27.07.2021)
Abstract : This note outlines a procedure for selecting radon alpha decay events in XENONnT based
on S1 signal information only. In order to distinguish the diﬀerent mono-energetic peaks which are
expected in the S1 spectrum as well as possible and to facilitate ﬁtting a model to the spectrum,
several signal corrections are derived based on information which is available in S1-only data. The S1
resolution for alpha events in the corrected spectrum amounts to ~1% or better. Besides simple
counting of alpha events to estimate radon rates inside the sensitive volume, which are predicted to
reach (1.75 +- 0.05) µBq/kg  in SR0 (corresponding of a reduction of 1.89 +- 0.09  via the RAD), the
techniques presented here also allow for investigating charge insensitive regions within the XENONnT
TPC.
Motivation
During RAD commissioning the anode was switched oﬀ due to liquid level variations for extended
periods of time. A radon event selection for data during that period consequently necessitates only
S1-based information to be used. For this reason, an S1-only framework is developed to analyze radon
rates and, here, applied to SR0 data to determine and predict the total reduction of the radon rate
achieved by the column.

import strax

class PeakPositionsWeighted (strax.Plugin):
    """
    Provides naive XY position estimates based on area-weighing.
    """
    provides = 'peak_positions_weighted'
    depends_on = ('peaks',)
    dtype = [('x_weighted' , np.float32, 'Reconstructed X position (cm),
uncorrected' ),
             ('y_weighted' , np.float32, 'Reconstructed Y position (cm),
uncorrected' )] + strax. time_fields
    __version__ = '0.0.7'
 
    def compute (self, peaks):
        result = np.ones(len(peaks), dtype=self.dtype)
        result ['time'], result['endtime' ] = peaks['time'],
strax.endtime(peaks)
 
        result ['x_weighted' ] *= float('nan')
        result ['y_weighted' ] *= float('nan')
 
        # weigh PMT positions with absoulte area observed in corresponding
PMT
        area_per_channel = peaks['area_per_channel' ]
        weight_map = np.abs(area_per_channel ) /
np.abs(area_per_channel ).sum(axis=1)[:, None]
 
        result ['x_weighted' ] = (weight_map *
straxen. pmt_positions ().x.values).sum(axis=1)
        result ['y_weighted' ] = (weight_map *
straxen. pmt_positions ().y.values).sum(axis=1)
 
        return result

S1 spectrum ﬁt
Applying all S1 area corrections and requiring S1 AFT to be within [0.15, 0.455] (see Fig. 18 for a map)
results in the spectrum seen in Fig. 19. It can already be seen that the peak positions relative to 222Rn
deviate further from their expected locations towards higher S1 areas when assuming the S1 area to
be directly proportional to the alpha energy. To remove the 210Po peak an upper threshold of 27 cm2 is
applied to the scaled naive r2 estimate (Fig. 18), which gives Fig. 20. A sum of ﬁve Gaussians, which
correspond to 222Rn, 218Po, 214Po, 220Rn, and 216Po each, is ﬁt to the resulting cleaner spectrum via a
minimum unbinned negative log-likelihood ﬁt. The relative resolution is assumed to be common for
every Gaussian besides the one used to model the 214Po peak, while the mean values and the relative
amplitudes are left to vary freely. 212Bi, which is within the region of the 218Po peak, is not included as
it is assumed to be negligible due to the more than factor ~10 diﬀerence in magnitude of the 220Rn
chain compared to the 222Rn chain together with the ~36% branching fraction of the 212Bi alpha decay.
Including the peak in the ﬁt also results in diﬃculties for the minimizer to arrive at a minimum for the
reasons outlined in the previous sentence.
The ﬁt (Fig. 21 and 22) yields a relative resolution of (0.84 +- 0.01) % for the 214Po peak and (0.886 +-
0.005) % for the others. While deviations from the Gaussian shape might result in the estimated
resolution to be better than it is, the ﬁt already indicates that a relative resolution of ~1 % is possible
at S1 sizes of O(10k PE). This bodes well for future high-energy analyses reliant on good energy
resolution, such as the search for the neutrinoless double-beta decay.

Radon rate estimation
To ﬁnally determine radon rates, the S1 spectrum ﬁt results are used to deﬁne counting regions for
each radon (daughter) nuclide. These are the 2 sigma intervals around each peak mean. Acceptance
losses caused by the selection of the counting regions are corrected for. Almost none of the peaks
that are accounted for overlap within these intervals, with the exception being 218Po and 220Rn. For
these, the overlap (less than 0.1% of leakage into each other's regions) is taken into account for both
the event number and its statistical error. The statistical error in this case is deﬁned as the central 1
sigma conﬁdence interval.
For the counting procedure itself, 12 subsequent runs (usually about 1 hour long) are combined per
counting period. The counts are then divided by the combined livetime according to the run dababase
entries to yield the rate per preiod. In order to ﬁnally determine the rate per mass, the ﬁducial mass
of the ﬁducial volume of this analysis has to be estimated. Assuming 214Po to be uniformly distributed
within the selected S1 AFT interval, the fraction of events left after applying the naive r2 threshold is
(22.6 +- 0.5) % when comparing the number of events within the threshold in the 214Po region passing
the 214Po cut (Fig. 23) to those which are outside the threshold (Fig. 24). The question remains about
the mass content of the [0.15, 0.455] S1 AFT region, which Florian Joerg has answered by mapping S1
AFT to the drift time using S1 + S2 information during similar light yield conditions as those in SR0.
His analysis gives a mass of (3.66 +- 0.09) t.
Combining everything together gives the rates for the 222Rn and 220Rn chain nuclides in Fig. 27 resp.
28. For the error bars, the statistical counting errors have been added linearly together with the errors
from the naive r2 threshold acceptance and the ﬁducial mass estimates. Similar to the radon analyses
of XENON100 and XENON1T it is found that only about half of the expected 214Po decays are seen. The
missing fraction is attributed to radon daughter plate out on detector components such as the
cathode. While the other 222Rn chain decays have rates of about 3.3 µBq/kg, 220Rn chain rates are at
about 0.1 µBq/kg or less.

Summary and outlook
S1-only information allows to achieve S1 resolution of ~1% for S1s at O(10k PE)
Radon rate converges to value of (1.75 +- 0.05) µBq/kg, compatible with previous reduction
factor estimates
S2 information still needed to determine ﬁducial mass for a given S1 AFT range
Possible future improvements:
Validate 214Po uniformity
Update ﬁducial mass value and error
Use precise conﬁdence interval for statistical error of volume correction instead of normal
approximation and error propagation
Account for inﬂuence of S1 peak ﬁt bounds on systematic eﬃciency correction
uncertainty
