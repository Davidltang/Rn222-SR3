Overview
To measure the solar-pp neutrino signal in the electron recoil (ER) region of interest (ROI), whose
signal model is shown in Fig.0.0 and will be updated by the theorists (see slides ), we would need to
ﬁgure out the background contribution in the ER region. The primary background contributors are
beta-emitters such as Pb214, Kr85, and Pb212 because their spectra are ﬂat in the ROI making these
backgrounds diﬃcult to distinguish from the signal. A good estimation on these backgrounds is
therefore critical if we want to detect any signals in the ER region.
This wiki-page focuses on Pb214 because as we can see from Fig.0.1b, it is the main background
contributor rate-wise. As it turns out, Pb214 is one of the decay daughter from the Rn222 decay
chain, which also include several mono-energetic alpha-emitters in the high energy region as shown
in Fig.0.1a. Since these beta-emitters and alpha-emitters originate from the same chain, these decays
are physically one-to-one corresponding to each other up to, for instance, the plate-out contribution.
The idea of the plate-out is such that only Rn222 is the noble gas and so can freely travel to the FV;
however, once Rn222 decays, the daughters are no long noble gas and tends to be lost to the
wall/electrodes. Assuming the plate out eﬀect is consistent within the experiment, we can use the
Pb214/alpha rate ratio to set a strict constraints on the Pb214 rate. The alpha spectra are shown in
Fig.0.2 with the two main alpha analysis methods to be covered in this note.

S1S2 Alpha Analysis
The S1S2 alpha analysis uses both S1 and S2. For this method, the mono-energetic alpha peaks are
shown in Fig.0.2a include Rn222-chain: Rn222 and Po218, while Rn220-chain: Rn220, Bi212, and
Po216. The S1S2 method has a diﬃcult time accessing the BiPo events such as Po214 and Po212,
mostly because of the possibility of multiple gammas emitted after a BiPo event. These gammas
make the S1S2 position construction inaccurate ( note) because it's diﬃcult to ﬁgure out which S2
peak the alpha belongs to. Therefore, instead of accurate determine the BiPo rates, the S1S2 analysis
focus on removing the BiPo leakage into other alpha peaks. On the other hand, Po210 is isolated
because of Pb210's long lifetime, meaning that it shouldn't be present in the ﬁducial volume. Finally,
because Po218 and Bi212 are too closed to each other in energy, their peaks can be diﬃcult to
separate. For this reason, this analysis works the best when either Rn222 or Rn220 is dominant.
During the Rn222 calibration and typical background runs, Rn222 will be dominant and will be the
main showcase for this work. Only during the Rn220 calibration would the Rn220 be dominant. The

The s1_area  corrections is done with the Rn222 data. The correction shows slightly better
performance in removing BiPo than cs1 (see plot) and will be unaﬀected by the LY correction. To do
this correction, ﬁrst apply all the cuts in the previous subsection, and ﬁt the following the empirical
function to s1_area  ,
s1_area1 = a*np.exp(-(b-s1_area_fraction_top)/c)*(1.0-np.exp(-(b-
s1_area_fraction_top)/c)) + d,
where a, b, c, d  are ﬁt parameters. The ﬁtted curve is shown as the solid orange curve in Fig.1.2a
middle-left plot. The dash orange borders are set manually to remove the events outside the borders
and so improve the ﬁt.
s1_corr1 = s1_area1/(A*np.exp(-(B-s1_area_fraction_top)/C)*(1.0-np.exp(-(B-
s1_area_fraction_top)/C)) + D)
for each events, where A, B, C, D  are now the ﬁtted parameters. Note, the same ﬁt is done with
drift_time  instead of s1_area_fraction_top  as shown in Fig.1.2a lower-left plot, but only one
such correction is needed.

S1only Alpha Analysis
The S1only alpha analysis skips the S2 information, whose mono-energetic alpha peaks are shown in
Fig.0.2b. The advantage of the S1only alpha analysis is that it has a handle to the BiPo events
because BiPo primarily aﬀects the S2 waveforms. This means that the rate of Po214 from the Rn222-
chain can also be tracked. This is especially helpful because Po214 comes after Pb214, which should
in principle be more aﬀected by the plate-out. In this way, we can have a rough but conservative
estimate of systematics coming from the plate-out eﬀects, between SR0 and SR1 say. The S1only
analysis has another advantage of being able to analyze the alpha data when the anode is oﬀ.
However, this ﬂexibility is more an advantage for monitoring than for science runs.
The S1only alpha analysis utilize the S1_cnn  position reconstruction that Matteo developed. This
position reconstruction performs better than naive S1 PMT pattern for XY and S1_AFT for Z. However,
in order to estimate the FV accurately, we would still need to rely on the matching between S1only to
S1S2 events. The disadvantage of the S1only is that the FV cut is less powerful than that of S1S2,
leaving a few more science events in the data.
Other than the quality cuts and FV cuts, the multipeak ﬁtting part of the S1only and S1S2 analysis are
essentially identical.

The ﬁducial volume estimation from s1only analysis essentially follow Dominick's note . The main idea
is to simply compare s1only events count to that of s1s2. The ﬁrst step is to match s1only event one-
to-one to that of s1s2; any events without s2 are ignored. Also because s1s2 doesn't quite handle

reference volume using s1s2 as shown in Fig.2.6a with a known uncertainty. Then if we make a s1only
ﬁducial volume cut, by comparing the count ratio:
(reference-volume)*(counts in reference-volume and s1only-FV)/(counts in
reference-volume),
should give us the estimated s1only FV with the uncertainty determined by Clopper-Pearson intervals
of a binary distribution (taking the larger side of the interval as the uncertainty).

Finally, the main goal of the radon analysis is to estimate the Pb214 background in the lowER. To do
that, we use the Rn222 calibration data, when the Pb214 contribution becomes orders of magnitude
higher than the other science events. Then we take the Pb214/Rn222 ratio, track the Rn222 in the
science runs, and use the same ratio to estimate the Pb214 count in the background runs
For this estimation, it is important that the cuts and lowER FV are ﬁnalized, otherwise this part of the
analysis need to be redone. It is also important that the runs/livetimes between the Pb214 and Rn222
data match. Notice however, the alpha analysis can be untouched and can have their own FV (as long
as no area corrections are updated). The reason is that the goal is getting the Pb214 counts in a ﬁxed
energy range, FV, and period of time, with the proportionality,
(Pb214 count) = (Pb214/Rn222 ratio)*(Rn222 count)
It does NOT matter where in the parameter space this Rn222 is obtained, as long as the
proportionality holds. Also for this reason, the errors coming from FVs of both Pb214 and Rn222
should not be included for this Pb214 estimation. Notice that the Pb214 count should NOT be
corrected by reconstruction eﬃciency and cut acceptance; the ﬁnal ﬁt templates should take this into
account.
The time-evolution aspect of the analysis is also important, since the chi2/ndof of the time-evolution
ﬁt would give up conﬁdence on whether this Pb214/Rn222 ratio  is Rn222 rate dependent or not,
assuming the contribution from the other background are constant in time. It would also be helpful to
include some background runs in the time evolution. Although their statistical error is large, it
provides additional information to the ﬁt. There are a two items regarding which energy range to use.
The ﬁrst is that the event from [0, 10]keV may have large tritium component for the background run,
and these event should not be included in this analysis. The second is that we did a AmBe calibration
during the decay phase of the Rn222 calibration, which activate the xenon. Especially the Xe133
component, it goes as low as ~70keV in range. These xenon activation event break the assumption
that the background is constant in time and should also not be included .

The rate of events within [10, 70]keV is shown in Fig.5.2a. Due to the activated Xe133 events >70keV
starting from AmBe calibration after run 51379  on 2023-03-28 10:34  LNGS time, and that [0,
10]keV can have tritium component for the background runs, the optimal range of [10, 70]keV is
chosen. Results from other ranges can be found in this note.
Each data point contains runs within 24 hours, unless the counts is smaller than 10 events. To avoid

Another item to take care of is the anti-correlation eﬃciency (ACE) dependence on both the run and
calibration. For details, see this note. Moreover, the alpha event reconstruction eﬃciency (EBE) also
has run dependence (see note). Since this dependence is a single scalar and we are after the
lowER/alpha, this alpha EBE correction can be done on lowER rate as well. These corrections are
performed at each data point, and the resulting diﬀerence can be found in Fig.5.2b.
Fig.5.2a: The top is showing the lowER spectrum in count, where the 10-70keV
is marked in blue. The bump starting from 70keV is coming from Xe133, the the
70keV choice is to avoid the bump. The bottom is showing the lowER rate
including the Rn222 calibration time period and background time period before
and after the calibration. The top spectrum includes events on from the runs
shown in the bottom plot.Fig.5.2b: The top shows the same lowER spectrum before (blue) and after (black) ACE+EBE
run/calibration dependence correction. The bottom shows the absolute count diﬀerence
(black-blue).
The lowER/Rn222 rate ratio ﬁt is done by ﬁtting the data with lowER = a * alpha + b  using the
conventional method with x- and y-error. The choice of method is Orthogonal Distance Regression
(ODR)  with the scipy.odr package . The advantage of ﬁtting a time evolution of the lowER/Rn222 rate
ratio is the discrimination power between a and b as Rn222 rate varies; these two variables are
otherwise indistinguishable if we combine all data at once.
The result is shown in Fig.5.4, with detailed numbers showing in the plot legends. The chi2/ndof is
given in the bottom legend, and the p-value is given on the top legend. The parameter a is given as
the ratio , and b as background . Notice that the unit conversion on the top legend and top right y-
axis are not completely accurate (because of using only Fig.6.1, but not Fig.6.2); they are there to
provide order-of-magnitude validity for the ﬁt. Note that the ﬁnal ratio need to include an additional
2% error from ACE+EBE for the calibration (see note), which will be separately added in the ﬁnal
section.

ratio is value to this second conversion factor.
