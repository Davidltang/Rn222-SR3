1. News from Operations Manager + Run Coordinator (Petr)
CRY/Water Cooling Loop:  The new alarms and automatic actions were tested thoroughly and
all are working as desired. New SC code was thoroughly checked.
CRY/Accumulator:  The SC code was successfully used to cool down and ﬁll about 150 kg. The
heat load into the accumulator was measured over a few days and is about 10 W. Today the
level raised to about 30 cm and LXe puriﬁcation was started on Wed. Apr. 29 noon. Current drift
time measured by Purity Monitor is ~ 580 µsec.
PUR:  Still re-circulating Xe gas.
TPC:  Electrodes HV test is starting on Monday May 4.
muVeto/nVeto:  Veto PMTs are ON 24/7 since a few weeks, with stable data taking; few tests
were performed to tune the digitizer threshold in some NV channels, lowering some of them to
15 ADC counts; preparation for the ﬁrst AmBe calibration (center of NV) completed (calibration
is scheduled today Thursday Apr. 30 in the afternoon).
RAD:  Main column cooled down and ﬁlled with ~50 kg of xenon. Currently operating in full
internal mode. The maintenance of all pumps is ﬁnished. A new charcoal puriﬁer was installed
to reduce the Rn-level of the puriﬁer. A GXe-only operation mode can be started at any time.
DST:  System is in standby waiting for the next distillation campaign. System is ready for any
distillation at any time. The main cold head lifetime comes to an end and cooling power is
loosing each week. The estimated end of lifetime is more likely to happen during the distillation
campaign. As an accident, the DST oﬀ-gas bottle LN2 level-meter was damaged. The repair is
ongoing.
SC/Upgrade-related:  Accumulator: New code was implemented and is operational. Future
extensions are foreseen (additional sensors and automatic procedures). Cold water loop: Code
was implemented and is operational. Level meter splitter: on hold (no new information). Gd
recovery plant: Interface to SC under discussion. Additional GdWLP sensors: Viewer and SCADA
screens updates are in progress.

Slow Control
CRY cold water commissioned. Three cooling modes available: “161”, “162”, and “168”. The
main cooling mode uses the LNGS cold water system via P161 or P162 (backup to each other). If
both fail, or if LNGS water is unavailable or too warm, the system can be switched to mode
“168” using our chiller skid. Automatic procedures are implemented to handle transitions
between P161 and P162, and to P168. See SCADA screen here. Viewer screen and CRY safety
screen updates are under construction.
During commissioning, experts are encouraged to disable alarms where appropriate.
Non-expert collaboration members who are curious to follow alarms are encouraged to use the
Slack channel for notiﬁcations and unregister from email/SMS alarm lists.
